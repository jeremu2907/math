# Electrical Properties of matter

## 1. Dielectrics, Polarization, and Permittivity

### Dielectrics
- Materials where its atoms and molecules dominant charges are *bounded* negatives and positive charges held in place by atomic and molecular forces.
- The charges are not free to travel. An ideal insulator does not have free charges.
- When external fields are applied, no charges will collect at the surface of insulators. However, centroids can shift slightly and creates electric dipoles.
- The formation of electric dipoles are also referred to as *orientational polarization*.

### Dipole Moments
- The effect of each electric dipole can be represented by a dipole moment $d\bold{p}_i$
$$
d\bold{p}_i = Q l_i \quad(C \cdot m)
$$
    - $Q$ is the magnitude of each of the negative and positive charge $(C)$
    - $l_i$ is the vectorial centroid displacement from (-) to (+) $(m)$

- The total dipole moment is:
$$
\bold{p}_t = \sum_{i = 1}^{N_e \Delta v}{d\bold{p}_i} \quad(C \cdot m)
$$
    - $N_e$ is the number of electric dipoles per unit volume $(m^{-1})$
    - $\Delta v$ is the volume $(m^3)$
- The *electric polarization vector* $\bold{P}$ is:

$$
\begin{aligned}

\bold{P} &= \lim_{\Delta v \rightarrow 0}{\frac{\bold{p}_t}{\Delta v}} \\\\
&= \lim_{\Delta v \rightarrow 0}{\frac{1}{\Delta v}\sum_{i = 1}^{N_e \Delta v}{d\bold{p}_i}} \quad (C / m^2)

\end{aligned}
$$

### Polarization

- Electric polarization for dielectrics can be produced by any of the following three types:
    - *Dipole / Orientational Polarization* is present in materials with permanent dipole moment that is randomly oriented even without external fields. When a field is applied, all the dipole tends to align.
    - *Ionic / Molecular Polarization* is present in materials with (-) and (+) ions. The ion bonds are displaced in opposite directions when a field is applied.
    - *Electronic Polarization* is present in most materials. The electron cloud is displaced relative to the nucleus when a field is applied.
- More than one type of polarization can occur at the same time.
